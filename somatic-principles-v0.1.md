# Somatic Principles for the Art Operation — v0.1 (draft)

*Historiotheque / Research. Drafted 2026-09-25 from the operator's notes.
Status: draft — open for revision. The shareable part of fifteen years of
Feldenkrais practice (on and off), the internal martial arts, Taoism, and
Stoicism — as they bear on the operation. What follows is not memoir; it is
the part that governs how the work gets done.*

## The premise

The operator is the instrument. Every procedure in the operation passes
through a body — through attention, breath, posture, fatigue. An Art
Operation that theorizes its archives but ignores its operator is theorizing
half the system.

## The Feldenkrais principles

- **Effortlessness / ease.** The right movement feels easy; strain is
  information, not virtue. Applied: a procedure that requires strain to
  execute is a procedure asking to be redesigned (cf. the error taxonomy,
  class 7: system errors — the flaw is in the design, not the person).
- **La plage d'aisance** — the range of ease (my teacher's French term). Work
  inside the range; expand it gradually from the inside; never force the
  boundary. Applied: the scale-down doctrine is *la plage d'aisance* at the
  scale of the whole operation — throttle before strain, not after collapse.
- **The kinesphere.** The space of possible movement around the body.
  Everyone has one; every workspace has one. Organize the workspace so the
  work lives inside the kinesphere, not at its edges (cf. class 6: workspace
  management errors — the tool where the hand expects it).
- **Reversibility.** Any movement you cannot reverse at will contains hidden
  strain. The test of a mature action is that you can stop it, undo it, do
  its opposite. Applied: prefer reversible procedures; distrust any step you
  cannot take back. Note the deliberate tension, held openly: the *record*
  is irreversible by doctrine (no backfill), while the *action* should stay
  reversible as long as possible. The log is the trace of actions that were
  free until they were taken.
- **Familiar, unfamiliar, strange.** In "mature behavior" (Feldenkrais's
  term), learning happens at the boundary between the familiar and the
  strange — approached with curiosity, never with force. The strange is not
  the enemy; the forced is. Applied: new methods are tried at small scale,
  inside the plage d'aisance, before they touch the operation.

## Tangent lines

Errors can take the form of tangent lines that never return: diverging paths,
mistaken roads taken, with negative impact on the Operator or the Operation.
The image matters because it distinguishes the error you can walk back from
(a detour) from the one you cannot (the tangent). Reversibility is the test;
the tangent line is what fails it. (Candidate illustration for error-taxonomy
classes 7 and 8.)

## The internal martial arts

Karate, Tai Chi Qigong, Aikido — and what I call the internal martial arts
generally. Beyond technique, what they taught:

- **Martial virtues** — not civic virtues. Discipline, timing, distance,
  reading the situation, economy of motion. "Martial" here does not mean
  waging war; it means the virtues of someone trained to meet force without
  being ruled by it.
- **Economy.** The smallest effective action. (Cf. the Taylorist audit of the
  studio: small costs add up — and small savings do too.)
- **Meeting force.** Aikido's lesson: don't oppose the incoming force; enter,
  blend, redirect. Applied: friction, failure, and interruption are not
  opposed — they are logged, studied, and redirected into the record.

## Taoism and Stoicism

- **Taoism — wu wei, effortless action.** The same truth as *la plage
  d'aisance*, in another tradition: act without forcing, and the action
  completes itself. The operation runs best when it is not driven.
- **Stoicism — the dichotomy of control.** Some things are up to the operator
  (the procedure, the log, the next move); most things are not (outcomes,
  reception, the weather). The audit trail is a Stoic document: it records
  what was in the operator's control, honestly, and lets the rest go. *Amor
  fati* — including the failures, which is why the releases are not brochures.

## The liminal space

Workspace and Lifespace cross over; they overlap. The body is the liminal
space between them — the instrument belongs to both. These principles live
there: they govern the operator, and the operator governs the operation.
