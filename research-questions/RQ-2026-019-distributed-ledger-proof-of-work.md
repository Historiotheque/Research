## Notes

**Core distinction (2026-09-27):** git already supplies the tamper-evident primitive blockchains are built on — every commit hashes its parent. What blockchains add is consensus and mining, which an art operation does not need. The proposed "proof of work" is therefore not cryptographic but literal: real human labor, publicly verifiable, performed at a cost. The "puzzle" is the work's own complexity — the aporia a reader experiences when the material exceeds what can be held in the head at once. Open sub-questions: does the existing verifiability stack already constitute the ledger, with nothing further to build? What would a non-cryptographic proof-of-work *protocol* look like for an art operation — what counts as a valid "block," and who or what verifies it? (Terminology note: not "blockchain" — the term carries cryptocurrency connotations the operation does not want.)
